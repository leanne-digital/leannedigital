Executive summary

October work has strengthened GBT’s technical SEO foundation. We shortened 125 lengthy titles, resolved a 22-page duplicate-title group, added two missing article descriptions and refined the privacy-policy and sitemap titles. The saved follow-up audit recorded 99/100; the later October 11 dashboard screenshot displays 100/100 with zero SEO findings.

We also completed the Google Search Console audit, obtained Google Business Profile access, and completed Bing Places setup and profile syncing. The internal-link audit produced 300 prioritized recommendations. On October 14, we implemented and verified 25 contextual internal links across 21 source pages; the remaining 275 recommendations are pending. Google Business Profile optimization, structured-data improvements and the AEO visibility review remain planned October work.

These results demonstrate improved technical audit health. They do not establish increased traffic, better rankings, more leads or improved AI-search visibility.

Internal linking

The audit reviewed 155 published URLs: 133 articles and 22 pages. It prepared 300 placements across 118 source pages, targeting 20 destination URLs: 172 high-priority service or industry placements and 128 medium-priority supporting-article or company-page placements. Priority reflects destination type, not measured rankings or traffic.

All three spreadsheet tabs were reviewed: Link opportunities (300 recommendations), Page inventory (155 published URLs), and Existing internal links (2,132 records). Existing source/destination pairs were excluded from new recommendations. As of October 14, rows 7–31 are marked Complete with dated verification notes: 25 contextual internal links across 21 source pages are implemented and verified live. The remaining 275 recommendations are pending.

The inventory covers extracted published page and article content. It excludes global navigation, header/footer templates, archives and other post types, so it does not establish which pages are orphaned. Existing-link HTTP statuses were not tested; the 20 recommended destinations were checked separately and returned HTTP 200 at the audit.
