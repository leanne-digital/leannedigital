OATLEY VIGMOND — SEO Report

AUGUST 2026

Monthly recap

August focused on improving the site’s technical foundation, crawl efficiency, content relationships, and semantic clarity. We cleaned up the XML sitemap, strengthened internal links across key legal content, refined legacy redirects, improved the homepage heading hierarchy, and enhanced the accessibility tree so search engines, screen readers, and AI-based agents can interpret the site more clearly.

Technical SEO

August’s audit maintains a 100/100 on-page SEO score, with organic monthly traffic at 6,109, 1,932 organic keywords, and 5,161 backlinks. The audit crawled 9,597 pages and identified 48 issues.

Technical work completed

Date Task completed What changed
August 2026 XML sitemap cleanup Audited redirected, outdated, and unnecessary URLs, then removed pages that should no longer be submitted to search engines.
August 2026 Internal linking improvements Strengthened links between key practice areas, expertise pages, lawyer profiles, and supporting content to improve crawl paths, authority flow, and topical relationships.
August 2026 Legacy redirect optimization Reviewed older and retired URLs and replaced broad or less-relevant redirects with the most appropriate current practice-area or expertise pages.
August 2026 Homepage heading structure Improved the H1/H2/H3 hierarchy with descriptive, search-focused headings that better communicate the firm’s services and expertise.
August 2026 Accessibility tree and AI crawler improvements Improved semantic page structure and accessibility markup so Google, screen readers, and AI-based agents can interpret primary content, headings, navigation, links, and page relationships more clearly.

Heading structure improvements

We corrected the heading structure across several priority pages. The updates establish one primary H1 per page, use H2 and H3 headings to reflect the true content hierarchy, and remove decorative heading levels that created noise for screen readers, search engines, and AI crawlers.

Next steps and strategy

In September, we will extend the internal-linking strategy beyond the initial Brain Injury and Catastrophic Injury work. We will identify additional high-value practice areas, expertise pages, lawyer profiles, and supporting resources, then build more relevant contextual links to strengthen crawl paths, topical authority, and the flow of page authority across the site.

We will continue the technical SEO and accessibility review by auditing heading structure across priority pages, correcting H1/H2/H3 hierarchy where needed, and identifying further crawl, redirect, sitemap, canonical, and indexability issues. This work will help Google, screen readers, and AI-based agents interpret the site’s primary content and page relationships more consistently.

We will review and strengthen structured data where it is appropriate, with particular attention to organization, legal-service, attorney, FAQ, article, and practice-area schema. We will also assess WebMCP readiness and opportunities so the site remains prepared for emerging AI-agent and tool-enabled search experiences while maintaining clear user controls and reliable information architecture.
