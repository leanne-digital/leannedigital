Web Designer vs. Web Developer: What’s the Difference

Leanne Jones October 9, 2025

Key Takeaways

  • A web designer focuses on what people see and how they experience your website.
  • A web developer focuses on how everything works behind the scenes.
  • The roles often overlap in areas like responsive design, accessibility, and performance.
  • When both skill sets work together, the result is a website that’s both beautiful and functional.
  • Leaning into strengths creates a smoother process, faster timelines, and stronger results.

When I first started designing websites, I tried to do everything myself. I’d design, build, troubleshoot, and fix every little thing behind the scenes. Over time, I realized something really important. The best websites are built through collaboration.

That’s when I started working with Gary, my developer partner. We each bring different strengths to the table. I focus on the design and user experience, and he makes sure everything runs smoothly behind the scenes. This balance has allowed us to create websites that not only look great but work beautifully.

If you’ve ever started a website project, you’ve probably heard the words web designer and web developer used like they mean the same thing. They don’t. But they do work closely together to make websites that not only look beautiful but actually work.

Since this is something I get asked about a lot, I want to break it down in a way that’s easy to understand and give you a little behind-the-scenes look at how me and Gary work together.

What I Do as a Web Designer

My work is all about designing with purpose. I focus on making a website easy to read, easy to navigate, and visually interesting so that my clients’ customers understand their message quickly.

For me, the research phase is where the magic starts. I love looking at other websites, browsing Pinterest, analyzing award-winning sites, and studying competitors. It honestly feels like walking through an art gallery, except I get to turn that inspiration into something real.

I’m a little more technical than the average designer, but I also believe time is valuable. I want to focus on the part I’m best at so my clients get my strongest work. That’s why I collaborate with Gary on the development side. Design after all is what I studied for 4 years in collage at Red River College Polytechnic.

What Gary Does as a Web Developer

One of the most common questions I get asked when I’m working with clients is “Who is Gary”

Gary is the techinal mastermind behind Leanne Digital. He tackles the things most people don’t see but are absolutely critical:

  • Technical integrations and troubleshooting
  • DNS and email issues
  • Website development and migrations
  • Form setups and error fixes

While I focus on what people see, Gary focuses on how everything actually works. It’s a powerful combo.

Where Design and Development Overlap

Here’s the thing. Designers often end up doing a bit of development, and developers often do a bit of design. The roles overlap, especially on things like:

  • Making sure the site looks great on mobile and desktop
  • Speed and performance
  • Accessibility and usability
  • How elements are structured and interact with users

But at the end of the day, designers think about the user’s experience, and developers think about the mechanics that make it all work smoothly.

How It All Started

Our very first project together was Bloc 15. This was our test run as a team.

Before Gary came on board, I was building websites very differently. He showed me how Astra and Elementor could give me more flexibility without limiting my design vision. I showed him how I design in Figma and how we could use it to collaborate seamlessly.

The project went so well that we kept going and eventually built sites like GBT Logistics
and Oatley Vigmond.
.

Why This Designer and Developer Duo Works

What makes our partnership strong is that we lean into our strengths. I can dive deep into design, storytelling, and UX. Gary can build out the technical side and make everything run like clockwork.

When both sides are doing what they’re best at, the results speak for themselves. Faster projects, cleaner builds, and websites that feel effortless for users.

Final Thoughts

If you’re starting a website project, understanding the difference between a designer and a developer can help you make smarter decisions and find the right people for the job.

A designer shapes the vision.
A developer builds the foundation.
Together, they make something powerful.

If you’re ready to bring your website idea to life, let’s chat. I’d love to show you what a good designer and developer team can do.
